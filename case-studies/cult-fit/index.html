Cult.fit: helping new members build the habit in their first 30 days

Most Cultpass buyers commit for a year upfront. This case study asks how the product can make sure that year gets used, so that renewal is earned, not hoped for.

RetentionOnboardingPrioritisationExperiment design

How to read this. An independent case study using public information only. I have not seen Cult.fit's internal data, so the problem is framed as a hypothesis, and the assumptions are labelled.

Two facts shape this problem. Cash arrives upfront because most buyers choose the 12-month plan. And a large share of new members arrive through someone they know. Both are useful for fixing habit formation.

2 · The problem

The year is sold on day one and won or lost in month one

Gym memberships have a well-known pattern: enthusiasm peaks at purchase and fades unless a routine forms. With a 12-month plan, a member who stops going in week three has already paid, so the problem stays hidden until renewal. That makes the early weeks the most valuable window the product has.

Hypothesis, stated openly

New members who book and attend several sessions in their first 30 days are much more likely to renew. Public data does not show attendance or renewal, so the first thing I would do in the job is test this against internal data. Everything below depends on it holding.

Who I am designing for (illustrative)

A first-time gym-goer in their late 20s who bought a 12-month plan after a trial class. They are motivated on day one, unsure what to book, and have no one to go with. A second user is a friend who joined through a referral. They have a built-in partner but nothing in the product that uses it.

4 · Recommendation

A guided first month, not a bigger catalogue

v1 scope

First 30 days

What ships
  • After purchase, the member answers three questions: goal, preferred time, and experience level.
  • The app proposes three sessions in week one that fit their calendar and nearby centre, and books them in one tap.
  • A buddy option lets the member invite a friend, or the person who referred them, to any booked session.
  • A weekly attendance streak replaces a daily one, because most members cannot go daily and a daily streak would punish normal behaviour.
Non-goals
No new content library, no pricing changes, no renewal offers, and no changes to the centre experience in v1.
Key assumption
Members would rather accept a good default than pick from the full schedule. That needs testing, since some will want control.

6 · How I would test it

Randomise by member, watch the capacity

  • Design: randomly assign new members to the guided month or the current experience. Run for about six weeks of new joiners, then follow each cohort for 90 days.
  • Read points: week 1 for bookings, day 30 for the north star, day 90 for retention. The renewal read comes much later, so day-90 activity is the proxy.
  • Capacity check: monitor the most popular slots daily. If defaults crowd them, spread suggestions across times and centres.
  • Segment cuts: referral joiners against others, centre formats, and first-time against returning members.
7 · Risks and open questions

What could break this

Risks

  • Popular slots overfill when many members get the same default.
  • Centres run under different models (company-owned, franchise and marketplace), so booking data and the experience may not be the same everywhere.
  • Members may see auto-booked sessions as pushy.

First questions for internal data

  • How strongly does first-month attendance predict renewal?
  • Where does the first-week drop-off happen: booking, travel or the first class?
  • Do referred members attend more, and do they attend together?
