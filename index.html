Project one · B2B SaaS

Threadline

Customer feedback in. A ranked roadmap out.

An AI copilot for product managers. It groups messy customer feedback by the need underneath, ranks themes by the revenue at stake, and drafts the PRD. Every judgement stays visible and editable.

Concept projectAI-nativeLive demoInvented data
Saved example output

Feedback inbox

Themes, ranked

score = ARR at stake × confidence ÷ effort

PRD draft

The bet

Show your work on every roadmap decision.

Feedback arrives from calls, tickets and renewals. The product manager has to turn it into a ranking that sales, engineering and the CEO will accept. Threadline does the tedious grouping and drafting with AI, and leaves each judgement call visible and editable.

The problem

Three things go wrong today.

It is scattered

Feedback sits in call notes, support tickets and CRM fields. Nobody sees all of it at once.

The loudest account wins

The most recent or most senior customer shapes the roadmap, whatever the pattern across accounts.

It is hard to defend

A ranking in a spreadsheet has no evidence trail, so every priority call turns into an argument.

These are hypotheses from how B2B teams commonly work. The first thing I would do is interview eight product managers and check them.

Who it is for

Two people share one decision.

Primary · hypothetical persona

Priya, product manager

Owns the roadmap at a 200-person SaaS company.

  • Needs: group feedback fast, explain the ranking.
  • Fear: missing a pattern, or ranking by gut feel.
Secondary · hypothetical persona

Arjun, head of sales

Brings customer requests to product every week.

  • Needs: see why a request ranked where it did.
  • Fear: a deal-blocking request sitting at the bottom.
How it works

Four steps, one human in charge.

  1. CollectFeedback with customer, plan and ARR attached to each item.
  2. GroupAI groups items by underlying need and proposes effort and confidence.
  3. RankPlain arithmetic turns those inputs into a score the PM can overrule.
  4. DraftAI writes a PRD from the evidence in the chosen theme.
The score

Arithmetic anyone can check.

The AI proposes the inputs. The formula is fixed and shown on screen, so the ranking can be questioned line by line.

$366k ARR at stake×0.9 confidence÷4 effort=82 · rank 1
$15k ARR at stake×0.5 confidence÷5 effort=1.5 · rank 6

Clause search has two requests and ranks last. Enterprise security has three requests worth $366k, all tied to renewals, and ranks first. The reasoning is on screen for sales to read.

Decisions

What I chose, and what I turned down.

The score is arithmetic

If a head of sales asks why a request ranked low, the answer has to fit on one line.

Turned down: a single AI priority score. It would be impossible to explain.

Every theme shows its evidence

Each theme lists the exact feedback items inside it and colors them in the inbox. A grouping you cannot audit will not be trusted.

Turned down: sentiment scores. They look precise and tell the PM very little.

Revenue gates are flagged

When an item says a renewal depends on a feature, the theme gets a visible flag so it cannot be buried by volume.

Turned down: treating all ARR as equal, which hides a deal that is about to churn.

The page works without AI

It opens on a saved example so a visitor always sees a real result. Live grouping is optional.

Turned down: an empty screen until the model answers.
Measuring it

One number, three inputs, two guardrails.

North star

Hours from raw feedback to a PRD the team reviews

Today this takes a day or more by hand.

Inputs

  • Grouping accuracy against hand-labelled items
  • Share of themes a PM accepts without regrouping
  • Share of PRD lines kept after review

Guardrails

  • Invented customers or numbers in a PRD: zero
  • Override rate on effort and confidence. A high rate means the proposals are off.
Quality

How I would evaluate the AI.

Grouping accuracy

Hand-label 50 feedback items, then measure how often the AI's themes match mine. Rerun after every prompt change.

Faithfulness

Every claim in a PRD must trace to an item id. Anything without evidence fails the check.

Calibration

Compare the AI's confidence with how often PMs actually accept the theme. High confidence with low acceptance means a prompt problem.

Checks already in the build

Each item sits in at most one theme, themes without valid evidence are dropped, and the PRD prompt forbids invented customers or numbers.

Risks

What could go wrong.

RiskHow the product handles it
A wrong grouping looks confidentEvidence is always visible and any score input can be overruled.
Large accounts dominate the rankingARR weighting is shown openly. A later version adds a request-count view beside it.
Customer feedback is sensitiveRedact names before sending to a model, and keep an audit trail of what was sent.

Sevanti Concierge

A concierge that builds the routine, and a dashboard that proves it.

An AI routine builder for a skincare brand. Shoppers answer five questions and get a bundle built only from the store's catalog. The merchant gets conversion, order value and swap data to judge it. Sevanti is an invented brand.

What shoppers change

Share of routines where the shopper swapped this product out.

Vitamin C and the bakuchiol serum are swapped most, mostly for price. A cheaper entry serum would likely lift bundle attach.

What shoppers typed in the free-text box

One in ten shoppers mentioned pregnancy or a skin condition. Those routines use gentle products and point to a dermatologist. The box is also free demand research for the brand.

The bet

Replace forty choices with one good answer.

A shopper lands on a skincare page with a wall of products and nobody to ask. The brand responds with discounts, which cut margin without helping the shopper decide. A short conversation that ends in a routine and a bundle helps the shopper first, and raises order value as a result.

The problem

Choice overload, from both sides.

Shopper

Too many options

They do not know which serum goes with which cleanser, so they leave or buy a single product.

Merchant

Discounts as the only lever

Small brands lean on offers to lift basket size, and margin pays for it.

Merchant

No proof for new tools

Every plugin promises more revenue. Without clean numbers, owners switch it off.

At Vudio.ai I worked with D2C brands every day and saw the third problem most. That is why the merchant view is half of this project.

Who it is for

A shopper and the person who runs the store.

Shopper · hypothetical persona

Ananya, 27

Combination skin, new to actives.

  • Wants: a routine she can trust in under a minute.
  • Fear: buying something that irritates her skin.
Merchant · hypothetical persona

Rohan, founder

Runs a ten-person skincare brand on a store platform.

  • Wants: higher order value without more discounts.
  • Fear: a tool that adds returns or complaints.
The journey

Five taps to a cart-ready routine.

  1. LandShopper opens a product page and sees the concierge prompt.
  2. AnswerSkin type, concern, routine size, budget and an optional note.
  3. BuildThe AI picks one product per step from the catalog, with a reason.
  4. CheckRules verify catalog, steps, budget and safety before it shows.
  5. BuyOne bundle price, with swaps allowed, then add to cart.
Safety

Treated as a requirement, not an afterthought.

If a shopper writes that they are pregnant, allergic, on a prescription or have a skin condition, the routine is limited to gentle products and says to check with a dermatologist. The rule runs in the page and in the prompt. The free-text note is also treated as information about the shopper and never as instructions to the model.

Four checks on every routine

  • Every product comes from the catalog
  • One product per step
  • Within the stated budget
  • Gentle products only when a risk is flagged

If a check fails

The page falls back to a simple rules engine, so the shopper still gets a valid routine. They never see an error or an unsafe pick.

Decisions

What I chose, and what I turned down.

A short form that ends in a routine

Shoppers on a product page want to tap, not type. Structured questions finish in under a minute and produce a cart-ready bundle.

Turned down: an open chat. It is slower, and the output is harder to turn into a cart.

The AI can only pick from the catalog

Every product id in the answer is validated. The model is a stylist working from this store's shelves.

Turned down: free recommendations from general knowledge, which could point to products the store does not sell.

The bundle discount is small and fixed

10 percent for three steps and 15 percent for four. The bundle is the better answer, and the discount only nudges it.

Turned down: deep, personalised discounts that train shoppers to wait for offers.

The merchant view ships on day one

Conversion, order value, funnel and swaps sit beside the shopper experience, because owners keep tools they can measure.

Turned down: shipping the shopper feature first and adding reporting later.
Measuring it

Revenue per session, with a seatbelt.

North star

Revenue per session, concierge against control

From a 50/50 split of sessions over eight weeks.

Inputs

  • Quiz completion rate
  • Routine added to cart
  • Bundle attach rate
  • Swap rate per product

Guardrails

  • Return rate does not rise
  • Complaint rate does not rise
  • Every flagged-risk session shows gentle picks and a professional note
Quality

How I would test the model.

A profile test set

About 40 shopper profiles, including sensitive skin, pregnancy, tiny budgets and contradictory answers. Check validity, steps, budget and the safety note.

Expert review

A dermatologist advisor reads a sample of routines each month and flags anything unwise.
