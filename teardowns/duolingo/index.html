Duolingo: how a daily habit became a business

A teardown of the streak loop. What brings 64.9M people back every day, where it strains, and two changes I would test.

RetentionHabit loopsGamificationExperiment design

How to read this. Independent analysis from public sources: company filings, earnings coverage and published commentary. Numbers are from the quarter ended 30 September 2026.

1 · The product in numbers

Users are growing faster than revenue

Daily active users64.9Mup 29% year on year
Paid subscribers13.1Mup 14% year on year
Revenue$309.0Mup 14% year on year
Bookings$316.1Mup 12% year on year

Source: Duolingo Q3 2026 shareholder letter and 10-Q.

The 10-Q says the rise in daily users came largely from better retention of existing users. That is the story of the product: the company grows by making today's users come back tomorrow, more than by finding new ones. The gap between user growth (29%) and paid subscriber growth (14%) is the tension I come back to later.

2 · The core loop

One small decision, repeated every day

Every part of the app serves a single question: will this person open it again tomorrow?

1LearnA short lesson with instant feedback. Low effort to start.
2EarnXP, gems and reward chests. Some rewards arrive at random, which keeps lessons feeling fresh.
3ProtectA streak counts consecutive days. A streak freeze forgives a missed day.
4CompeteWeekly leagues rank people by XP, so a quiet day costs rank.
5ReturnReminders in a playful brand voice pull people back before the day ends.

Each pass makes tomorrow's choice easier: skipping now costs a streak, a league position and a small piece of identity.

3 · What works, what strains

Strong at retention, softer at depth

What it does well

  • It found the right lever. The growth team reportedly saw that improving retention of current users moved daily users more than acquiring new ones, then focused there.
  • Loss aversion with a safety net. The streak creates pressure, and the freeze removes the catastrophic failure that makes people quit for good.
  • Experiment speed. The CEO said AI is shortening the time from idea to live test, which compounds the retention work.
  • Cost curve turned into product. Management said AI video calls became cheap enough to offer to every Super subscriber, reportedly from about 30 cents to under 1 cent a call.

Where it strains

  • Showing up is not the same as learning. Commentators note that people can protect a streak with the shortest lesson, so the headline metric can drift from the outcome people want.
  • Paid growth lags usage. Daily users accelerated while paid subscriber growth kept slowing, and management expects it to bottom out in a couple of quarters.
  • Packaging is still moving. The top Max tier is being retired and cheaper tier tests were reported as not home runs, so the paid ladder is not settled.
4 · What I would test

Two changes, each with a way to be wrong

RetentionDepth

Depth-aware streaks

A streak rewards presence, so a one-minute session protects it as well as a full lesson. I would keep the streak exactly as it is and add an optional layer on top.

Idea
After several days of minimum-effort sessions, offer a short optional stretch round. Completing it earns a bonus such as a streak freeze or a cosmetic. The streak itself is never gated.
Hypothesis
Users who complete stretch rounds retain better at day 60 than similar users who only do minimum sessions, because they feel progress, not just a number.
Primary metric
Day-60 retention for users with a long streak.
Secondary
Lessons completed per week, skills progressed, stretch round completion rate.
Guardrails
Daily active users, session start rate, streak-loss churn. If starting a session gets harder, the test fails.
Test design
A/B test with a holdout, run on users with 14+ day streaks and recent minimum sessions. Four weeks to read engagement, eight to read retention. The thresholds (14 days, "minimum session") are my assumptions and would come from data first.
Risk
It adds friction to the app's best habit. That is why it is optional, and rewards are positive, never penalties.
OnboardingMotivation

A readiness view tied to the learner's real goal

People start with a reason, such as a trip, a job or family. After a few weeks the daily task can feel disconnected from that reason, which is when motivation tends to fade.

Idea
Keep the stated goal visible after day one with a simple readiness view: "you can now order food and ask for directions". Daily lessons visibly move it.
Hypothesis
Linking daily effort to a real-world outcome reduces drop-off in the mid-funnel window, roughly days 14 to 45.
Primary metric
Day-30 and day-60 retention for new users in the test.
Guardrails
Onboarding completion, day-1 retention, lesson completion rate.
Test design
New-user A/B test with the readiness view as the variant. Before building, I would check how many users give a goal, and whether stated goals predict retention at all.
Risk
Readiness claims must be honest. An inflated readiness score would break trust the first time someone tries to order dinner.
5 · How I would measure it

Metrics to watch, and how each can mislead

MetricWhy it mattersWatch out for
Retention by cohort (D1, D7, D30, D60)The core health signal. Daily user growth is mostly a retention story.Averages hide cohorts. Read new and existing users separately.
Retention of current usersThe lever the company says moves daily users most.Gains from streak pressure can reverse when novelty fades.
Lessons or minutes per active dayA proxy for depth that the streak count cannot show.More minutes is not always better learning.
Progress signal (skills or levels advanced)Connects engagement to what learners want.Needs a definition that cannot be gamed.
Paid conversion and renewalTurns daily users into revenue, where growth is slowing.Aggressive paywalls can hurt the free experience that fuels retention.
The takeaway for a PM

Duolingo shows how far a single well-chosen metric can carry a product. It also shows the cost: when the metric is "did they show up", the next job is making sure showing up still means progress. The best next experiments sit in that gap.
